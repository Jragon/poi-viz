## A) Executive synthesis (1–2 pages)

### What the doc is trying to accomplish

**What the doc says:**
Drex is aiming for two things: (1) teach poi spinners how to model poi paths with basic trig; (2) build a **formal, falsifiable classification scheme** for poi patterns based on parameter ranges, not vibes or regional naming drift (p. 3–4).
Scope is intentionally restricted: single-poi patterns “sans the body”, and explicitly _not_ covering hybrids/inversions/timing+direction (p. 3–4).
He also states limitations: ignores gravity (mostly), and the math is better for “complete patterns” than partial/composite sequences; complexity “increases dramatically” as you chain moves (p. 4–5).

**My V2 interpretation:**
This is a classification paper disguised as a tutorial. Its strongest contribution to V2 is _not_ the exact equations—it’s the **parameter-to-family mapping** and the insistence that definitions should be testable. But it’s also deeply “cycle-first”: a lot of its definitions assume closed paths over (t \in [0,2\pi]) (or multiples), which is useful for cataloguing patterns, but actively hostile to a sequencer-first system unless you explicitly **piecewise-ize** it.

### Central abstraction

**What the doc says:**
The core move is: model poi as **parametric curves** generated by sums/products of sin/cos terms.

- 2D flowers/unit-circle patterns: handpath circle + poi rotation relative to it (p. 12–19).
- Third-order motions: add a third centre of rotation (another sin/cos term) (p. 20–24).
- 3D manifolds (weaves/body tracers): introduce a (z) term (often (z=\sin t)) to create depth/plane-bends (p. 24–30).
- Toroids: special 3D forms with plane rotation, with separate isobend/antibend/probend families (p. 33–47).
  Then he introduces a “universal/generalized equation” with lettered parameters to cover the families in the paper (p. 48–50).

**My V2 interpretation:**
The central abstraction is **harmonic/epicycloid composition**: “poi path = sum of rotating vectors (and sometimes a rotating plane)”. That maps cleanly to your **node-rig + transport** approach if you treat each trig component as an **explicit segment generator** (with boundaries) rather than “the truth”.

### What makes it valuable relative to other poi theory

**What the doc says:**
Value claims: (a) you can generate new patterns by exploring parameter space; (b) you can define family boundaries (“ranges of values”) and settle vocabulary arguments with falsifiability (p. 3–4).

**My V2 interpretation:**
High value for V2:

1. **Parameter ranges as definitions** (flowers vs unit circle vs third-order vs manifolds vs toroids), which is directly usable as overlay classifiers.
2. **Lobe/antilobe framing** as _transition anchors_ (p. 59–60), which is basically a “nexus-point” theory in disguise.
   Low value for V2 core:

- It’s not a sequencing model. When it touches sequencing (composites/CAPs/stalls), it immediately becomes piecewise and admits it’s ugly/hard (p. 51–56). That is exactly where your V2 architecture should take over.

## B) Vocabulary and primitives glossary

> Format: **Term (doc)** → definition (doc) → mapping to V2 (rig/segment/plane/transport) → pitfalls

### Unit circle (doc)

**Doc:** Unit circle is used as the base parametric circle; Drex uses a **nonstandard convention**: swaps sine/cosine axes and prefers clockwise build for aesthetics (p. 11).
**V2 mapping:** This is **coordinate convention**, not a concept. In V2, store convention centrally and never bake it into generators.
**Pitfalls:** If you import his equations literally, you inherit his axis swap + clockwise preference and will get “phase-0 confusion” instantly (p. 11).

### Handpath / core handpath

**Doc:** The “circle traced by the hand” (2D flowers), sometimes idealised as point/line (static spin; weaves) (p. 12–14, p. 26).
**V2 mapping:** Node rig: **Hand node** path in a segment, with explicit radius/centre. For weaves where he reduces it to a line, that’s an **authorial simplification** you should encode as a generator option, not assume.
**Pitfalls:** His later classification uses performer armspan constraints (p. 60–62). That’s embodiment creeping into what’s otherwise pure geometry.

### Downbeats (d, m, n, etc.)

**Doc:** “Number of ‘down’ beats of poi relative to handpath’s downbeats” for flowers; later generalised as beat-count multipliers on (t) (p. 14–19, p. 26).
**V2 mapping:** Transport overlay: a **frequency ratio** between components (hand angular rate vs head angular rate vs plane rotation rate).
**Pitfalls:** “Downbeat” is coordinate/phase dependent; he even admits Cartesian “down” overgeneralisation (p. 14). In 3D and plane-embedded segments, “downbeat” must be defined relative to a plane frame, not global Y.

### Phasing (p, q, c, d offsets)

**Doc:** Offset terms that shift where the curve starts (e.g., (+\pi) hacks for petal placement) (p. 9–10, p. 23).
**V2 mapping:** Segment parameter: **phase offset** (radians) for each oscillator component, plus explicit boundary state (start pose).
**Pitfalls:** Drex mixes “phasing” as sign flips (±1) _and_ additive offsets (e.g., (+\pi)); you must separate **sign (direction)** from **phase offset**.

### Inspin / Antispin

**Doc:** Sign of the poi multiplier relative to hand determines inspin vs antispin (2D flowers: positive → inspin, negative → antispin) (p. 15–16). Later formalised in parameter constraints (p. 60–61).
**V2 mapping:** Segment semantics: **relative angular velocity sign** between hand→head rotation and handpath rotation.
**Pitfalls:** This depends on coordinate handedness + “clockwise preference” (p. 11). In V2 it must be defined relative to a **right-handed frame** and a stated view direction.

### Petal vs lobe/antilobe

**Doc:** He avoids “petal” ambiguity and instead uses **lobe / antilobe** based on whether hand and poi velocity vectors are parallel in same vs opposite direction; these are gradients, not binary (p. 59–60).
**V2 mapping:** These are **nexus candidates**: states where relative velocity alignment hits extrema, useful as natural boundary points for segment transitions and compilation constraints.
**Pitfalls:** Velocity-vector definitions assume differentiability and continuous time; piecewise segments must either enforce tangent continuity or explicitly tolerate discontinuities.

### Third-order motions

**Doc:** Add a third centre of rotation (elbow/shoulder or virtual) → add a third trig term; multiple families (fractal flowers, triquetra expansions, etc.) (p. 20–24, p. 62–64).
**V2 mapping:** Node rig extension: add an optional **extra node** (e.g., elbow proxy) or treat it as **handpath generator** feeding the hand node.
**Pitfalls:** The doc blurs physical joint centres with “virtual centres” (p. 20). In V2 you should treat these as _generators_ that produce a handpath curve; do not pretend you’re modelling anatomy unless you deliberately choose to.

### Manifolds / plane bends / plane breaks

**Doc:** Many 3D patterns use “plane bends”; he calls the family “manifolds” (weaves, tracers, toroids) (p. 24). Simple weaves are modelled by adding a sinusoid in (z) (p. 25–26).
**V2 mapping:** This is directly compatible with **planar internal motion + 3D embedding**, but: Drex’s method is “global 3D parametric”, whereas you want **explicit plane frames per segment** and explicit plane transitions at nexus points.
**Pitfalls:** He says axes are “agnostic” to body (p. 24), but then also uses “behind the body” language (p. 27–28). That ambiguity matters for any UI/teaching overlay.

### Toroids (isobend / antibend / probend; grace beats)

**Doc:** Toroids are “most complex”; require plane-bend logic and special equations; three types depend on how the poi plane rotates relative to handpath (p. 33–47).
**V2 mapping:** Toroids should be **generators** that emit piecewise planar segments with explicit plane frame rotation; “grace beats” are explicit boundary features.
**Pitfalls:** Toroid equations are multiply-nested trig products (p. 46–47) and are numerically/mentally opaque; for sequencing, you care more about **topology + boundary alignment points** than the exact closed-form.

## C) Motion representation taxonomy

### 1) Harmonic / parametric (dominant)

**What’s explicit vs implicit:**

- Explicit: (x(t),y(t)) (and sometimes (z(t))); frequency multipliers; phase offsets (p. 12–24, p. 24–30).
- Implicit: segment boundaries (until composites); continuity assumptions; frame conventions (except the unit-circle note) (p. 11, p. 51–56).
  **Global vs local:** Global; generally assumes one equation generates the whole pattern over (t\in[0,2\pi]) or a multiple (p. 11, p. 51).
  **Periodicity assumptions:** Strong: patterns are defined as returning to origin at (t=0) (p. 51).

**My V2 interpretation:**
Use this as a **generator language**: “pattern family → oscillator stack → emitted segments”. Don’t let it dictate the core representation.

### 2) Geometric/topological (secondary)

**What the doc says:**
He uses topology metaphors: “manifolds”, “self-intersecting plane / real projective plane” for weaves (p. 24–26).
**My V2 interpretation:**
This is valuable as an **analysis overlay**: classify emitted motion by topology, identify crosspoints/nexus points.

### 3) Symbolic / classificatory (parameter-range schema)

**What the doc says:**
In the definitions section, families are defined by parameter constraints (e.g., inspin vs antispin; unit circle; third-order) plus some physical constraints like “gravity exists” bounding |n| (p. 60–62).
**My V2 interpretation:**
This is basically your overlay system: “given a segment stack, classify it”.

### 4) Piecewise/state-machine (appears only when forced)

**What the doc says:**
Composite patterns (CAPs, stalls, composite toroids) require splitting time ranges and stitching different equations (p. 51–56).
**My V2 interpretation:**
This is where V2 should replace the doc: your piecewise-first design is the correct generalisation of the doc’s “uh… do two halves and glue them”.

## D) The canonical “core model” if any

### What the doc says (core model)

For flowers/unit-circle (2D), he arrives at:

- (x = (h\sin t) + p(\sin(dt)))
- (y = (h\cos t) + p(\cos(dt)))
  with (h)=handpath radius, (p)=initial phasing, (d)=relative downbeats (p. 19).
  For third-order motions, add another term:
- (x = (h\sin t) + p(\sin(dt)) + q(\sin(ft)))
- (y = (h\cos t) + p(\cos(dt)) + q(\cos(ft)))
  with (q)=poi phasing, (f)=poi downbeats (p. 24).
  For simple weaves: (x=\sin(dt),y=\cos(dt),z=\sin t) (p. 26).
  For toroids, a distinct model:
- (x = \sin t + (\cos(ct+d))\sin(ft))
- (y = \cos t + (\cos(ct+d))\cos(ft)) (p. 46)
  and later constraints linking petal count to the plane-rotation parameter (p. 47).

### My V2 translation

#### State variables (V2-canonical)

Per segment:

- **Transport interval**: ([T_0,T_1]) in seconds, plus mapping to local phase (\phi\in[\phi_0,\phi_1]).
- **Plane frame**: a rigid transform defining the segment’s plane in 3D (wall/wheel/floor), plus (optional) a plane-frame angular velocity if the plane itself rotates.
- **Node rig params**:
  - handpath oscillator(s): amplitude (radius), frequency, phase offset
  - head relative oscillator(s): amplitude (poi length), frequency, phase offset
  - optional extra oscillators for third-order (interpreted as additional centre-of-rotation generators)

#### Invariants (useful)

- **Rig length constraint** (motion-model version): head is always at fixed distance from hand in the segment’s plane model (unless you explicitly allow “string compression” as stylisation). Drex assumes this implicitly.
- **Closure is not an invariant** in V2. Drex makes closure part of the definition of “pattern” (p. 51). V2 should treat closure as a _property_.

#### Derived vs chosen-by-convention

- Chosen: coordinate handedness; phase-zero; view direction; “clockwise is positive?”
- Derived: lobe/antilobe events (from velocity alignment); petal count relationships (e.g., (p=|1-d|) in 2D flowers, p. 17).

## E) Timebase and sampling

### What the doc says

- Uses (t) as a continuous parameter, typically (t\in[0,2\pi]) (p. 11).
- Patterns often defined as returning to origin when (t) completes its interval; some require larger intervals (p. 51).
- For composites, he explicitly splits (t) into subranges (e.g., CAP side1 (t\in[0,\pi]), side2 (t\in[\pi,2\pi])) (p. 52).
- No real sampling discussion; implicit assumption: continuous curves, differentiable enough for “smoothness”, but he admits stalls are hard and his modelling may be “good enough” at best (p. 53–55).

### Compatibility with deterministic seek/scrub + piecewise segments (V2)

**Works well if you do this:**

- Define a single transport time (T) (seconds).
- For each segment, define a deterministic mapping (T \mapsto \phi) (phase), usually linear, but allow non-linear time-warp as an explicit segment feature (needed for “stall-ish” effects—see p. 54).
- Avoid “pattern = closed cycle” as a requirement; treat closure as an analyzable property.

**Sceptical note:** The doc uses “beats” in several incompatible senses (downbeats, plane beats, poi beats) across 2D/3D/toroids (p. 17, p. 26, p. 47). In V2, you’ll need a strict ontology: **frequency multipliers per oscillator per plane**, full stop.

## F) Phase, reference frames, and coordinate conventions

### What the doc says

- He explicitly rejects the standard unit-circle convention, swapping sine/cosine axes and preferring clockwise orientation, for aesthetics and personal dominant spin direction (p. 11).
- Inspin vs antispin is tied to the sign of the multiplier in the poi term (p. 15–16).
- He uses phase offsets like (+\pi) as an ad hoc tool to place petals (p. 23).

### My V2 convention proposal (single clean convention)

Goal: kill “phase 0 confusion”, support wall/wheel/floor, be diagrammable.

**Convention (V2):**

1. **Right-handed 3D world frame**: (+\hat{x}) right, (+\hat{y}) up, (+\hat{z}) toward viewer (or away—pick one and lock it).
2. Each segment has a **plane frame** (P):
   - plane normal (\hat{n})
   - plane axes (\hat{u},\hat{v}) in-plane, right-handed with (\hat{u}\times\hat{v}=\hat{n}).

3. **Planar phase (\phi)** is measured in the plane frame:
   - (\phi=0) points along (+\hat{v}) (“up” in the plane diagram).
   - (\phi) increases with right-handed rotation from (\hat{v}) toward (\hat{u}).

4. Define **inspin/antispin** as the sign of **relative angular velocity** between head-around-hand and hand-around-centre, both measured in the plane frame.
5. When presenting 2D diagrams, always state the viewing direction (looking along (+\hat{n})).

**Why this beats Drex’s approach:** It separates (a) aesthetics (where you want the triquetra to “point”) from (b) the physics-free mathematical model. Drex bakes aesthetics into the coordinate system (p. 11), which is fine for a PDF, but poison for an engine.

## G) 3D implications

### What the doc says

- He adds (z) terms to create depth and describes many 3D patterns as requiring “plane bends” (p. 24).
- Simple weaves: (z=\sin t) while (x,y) complete multiple cycles (p. 25–26).
- Body tracers: 2D flower in (x,y) plus (z=\sin t) to put petals “behind” and “in front” (p. 27–30).
- Toroids: explicitly about rotating the poi plane through a torus-like surface; he distinguishes isobend/antibend/probend by plane rotation behaviour relative to handpath (p. 33–34).

### Mapping to V2 planar-internal + plane embedding

**Direct fit:**

- Treat Drex’s 3D as a _signal_ that the motion is not confined to a single plane; in V2 encode that as **segments whose plane frames evolve**, or sequences of planar segments connected at nexus points.

**Design options (explicit):**

1. **Plane-frame-per-segment (recommended default):**
   - Most segments are planar; “3D” is an ordered list of planar segments with different plane frames.
   - Plane transitions occur at nexus points with explicit constraints (intersection line, continuity requirements).

2. **Continuously rotating plane within a segment (needed for toroids/weaves-as-surfaces):**
   - Plane frame is time-varying inside the segment (e.g., rotate normal around a path).
   - This matches Drex’s toroid intuition but must be first-class, not a hack.

3. **Fully 3D parametric segment (quarantine):**
   - Allow a segment to define (x(t),y(t),z(t)) directly.
   - Useful for importing the paper’s equations verbatim, but risks becoming “physics/parametric soup” again.

**Sceptical note:** Drex says axes are “agnostic” to body orientation (p. 24), but then relies on body-relative interpretation (“behind the performer”) for tracers (p. 27–28). V2 should make body-relative meaning an overlay layer (camera + avatar), not part of core motion.

## H) Patterns, families, and generators

Below: family → doc parameters/constraints → invariants → V2 role

### Flowers (2D)

**Doc:** Handpath circle + poi circle; petals relationship (p=|1-d|) (p. 17), inspin/antispin by sign (p. 15–16).
**Invariants:** closed curve (within chosen (t) window), consistent lobe/antilobe alternation (p. 59–60).
**V2 role:** **Generator** emitting planar segments; also **classifier overlay** (“this segment stack is a flower family member”).

### Unit circle patterns

**Doc:** subset of flowers; handpath radius (b=1/2), single poi beat (|n|=1) (p. 61–62).
**Invariants:** one-beat structure; includes extension/isolation/cateyes (p. 61).
**V2 role:** generator presets + teaching UI layer (because it’s a crisp constrained family).

### Third-order motions

**Doc:** add a third centre; constraints (a>0, |b|>0, a+|b|\le 4) etc (p. 62). Subfamilies: fractal flowers, triquetra expansions with explicit derived relations (p. 63–64).
**V2 role:** generator that builds **handpath-from-handpath** stacks (i.e., rig extensibility); also analysis overlay linking to CAP-like segmentation.

### Simple weaves (manifolds)

**Doc:** (x,y) cycle (d) times while (z=\sin t) once; notes body/orientation ambiguity (p. 26–27).
**V2 role:** generator that emits either (a) rotating-plane segments or (b) multi-plane piecewise segments, depending on your chosen representation.

### Body tracers

**Doc:** 2D flower + (z=\sin t) to shift petals in depth (p. 27–30).
**V2 role:** overlay/generator hybrid: the “tracer” meaning depends on body placement, which the doc admits is out of scope elsewhere (p. 4).

### Complex weaves

**Doc:** described as “flowers bent around the body”; equation form given (p. 30–32).
**V2 role:** likely **quarantine** as generator until you have body/obstacle overlays; otherwise classification will be mush.

### Toroids (isobend/antibend/probend; grace beats)

**Doc:** most complex; distinct equations and rules, including petal relationships tied to plane rotation (p. 33–47).
**V2 role:** generator that emits (1) planar arcs + (2) explicit plane transitions at corners (“grace beats” as nexus events).

## I) Transitions and sequencing

### What the doc says

- He defines a “pattern” as leaving and returning to origin at (t=0) (p. 51).
- Composite patterns are transitions between incomplete elementary patterns (p. 51).
- CAPs are explicitly made by **splitting two elementary patterns in half and gluing them**, with time-range boundaries stated (p. 52).
- He notes modelling stalls accurately is extremely hard; solutions are complex/time-warp-ish and may not be worth it (p. 54–55).
- Composite toroids: he ends up using **multiple equations (segments)** and admits mismatch between segments because real spinners “make constant adjustments” (p. 56).

### V2 compile/validate model (translation)

**Segment boundary constraints**

1. **Position continuity** at boundaries: required by default.
2. **Tangent continuity** (velocity direction/magnitude): optional constraint level; needed if you want “flow clean” joins.
3. **Plane continuity**: either same plane frame, or explicit plane transition with constraints (intersection line, allowed instantaneous rotation, etc.).

**Explicit failure modes (no silent kludges)**

- **Unreachable join**: end pose and start pose mismatch beyond tolerance.
- **Plane mismatch**: end plane frame incompatible with next segment without a nexus transition.
- **Phase aliasing**: same visible curve, different internal phase state (classic “looks right, compiles wrong”).
- **Embodiment dependency**: transition only works if you assume body/obstacle avoidance not modelled (doc repeatedly warns about body scope, p. 4, p. 27).

**Diagnostics UI should show**

- At each boundary: pose delta (hand pos, head pos), tangent delta, plane delta.
- Candidate “natural join points”: lobe/antilobe alignments (p. 60), “grace beats” in toroids (p. 44–47), symmetry points.

## J) Relationship to QFT / CAPs / VTG / Symmetry / Hybrid-relation math

### Overlaps / new / conflicts (high level)

**QFT (harmonic/phase lens)**

- Overlap: everything here is harmonic composition; explicit frequency ratios and phases (p. 12–24).
- New: explicit **parameter-range definitions** and a big practical library of family recipes.
- Conflict: Drex’s coordinate/clockwise choices (p. 11) are “local conventions”; QFT wants canonical invariants.

**CAPs**

- Overlap: CAPs appear explicitly as composite patterns built from partial cycles (p. 51–53).
- New: his CAP modelling is literally “piecewise time-domain stitching” with direction reversal for retracing handpath (p. 53).
- Conflict: CAPs in V2 should be first-class segment assemblies, not “two halves of a universal equation”.

**VTG / symmetry rule overlays**

- Overlap: lobe/antilobe as shared transition anchors (p. 60) is basically “symmetry points are easy transitions”.
- New: some toroid families correspond to polygon/star symmetries (p. 40–45, p. 56).
- Conflict: doc sometimes treats “aesthetic petal placing” as a goal requiring phase hacks (p. 23); symmetry overlays should derive those systematically.

**Hybrid-relation math (relation signatures)**

- Overlap: parameter sets (\rightarrow) family label is a relation signature.
- New: explicit constraints like “gravity implies (|n|\ge 1)” (p. 61) can become a “physical plausibility” flag in relation signatures.
- Conflict: many different patterns can be “orientation ambiguous” without body context (p. 27). Relation signatures must carry “requires embodiment context” metadata.

### Translation table (doc → V2 canonical → optional equivalents)

- **“Downbeats (d)” (flowers)** → **frequency ratio (head-around-hand vs hand-around-centre)** → QFT harmonic ratio
- **Phasing (p,q)** → **phase offsets per oscillator** → QFT phase offsets
- **Inspin/antispin** → **sign of relative angular velocity** → symmetry: handedness label
- **Lobe/antilobe** → **velocity alignment extremum events (nexus candidates)** → CAP boundary candidates; VTG “cardinals”
- **Composite/CAP** → **segment assembly with explicit boundary constraints** → CAPs (direct)
- **Manifolds/weaves** → **multi-plane embedding or rotating plane segments** → symmetry/topology overlay
- **Toroid type (iso/anti/pro)** → **plane-frame rotation mode** → symmetry: polygon/star families

## K) What to steal (high leverage)

1. **Parameter-range-as-definition** (p. 60–64): makes overlays falsifiable; fits your “not core truth” principle. Misapply it and you’ll hardcode body/armspan constraints into core.
2. **Explicit coordinate-convention disclosure** (p. 11): steal the _honesty_, not the convention. Misapply it and your engine becomes person-specific.
3. **Lobe/antilobe framing as transition points** (p. 60): perfect for nexus/boundary discovery. Misapply it and you’ll assume smooth differentiability everywhere.
4. **Admitting sequence modelling is hard** (p. 4, p. 51–56): validates your sequencer-first approach. Misapply it and you’ll ignore useful partial-cycle structure.
5. **Third-order as “add a centre”** (p. 20–24): matches rig extensibility. Misapply it and you’ll confuse virtual vs anatomical centres.
6. **Weaves as plane-bend phenomena** (p. 24): supports plane-first schema. Misapply it and you’ll treat (z=\sin t) as “the weave truth”.
7. **Toroid taxonomy (iso/anti/pro)** (p. 33–34): strong family split for generators. Misapply it and you’ll import opaque equations rather than using structural constraints.
8. **Grace beats as structural features** (p. 44–47): boundary anchors for transitions. Misapply it and you’ll reify a stylistic term into physics.
9. **Composite toroids done as multiple segments** (p. 56): aligns with piecewise-first. Misapply it and you’ll accept ugly joins without diagnostics.
10. **“Gravity exists” constraint flag** (p. 61): useful as plausibility overlay. Misapply it and you’ll block stylised/nonphysical motion modelling.

## L) What to reject or quarantine

1. **Cycle-closure as the definition of “pattern”** (p. 51): reject as core. Keep as an analyzable property. Otherwise sequencing gets boxed into loops.
2. **Baking aesthetics into coordinates** (p. 11): quarantine as display preference. Otherwise every generator inherits hidden axis swaps.
3. **“Downbeat” as a universal primitive** (p. 14, p. 17): quarantine; in 3D it becomes ambiguous. Use oscillator frequency ratios instead.
4. **Body-language without body model** (p. 27–28): quarantine into teaching overlays. Otherwise you’ll bake “behind the body” into math that claims to be body-agnostic (p. 24).
5. **Stall modelling inside trig soup** (p. 54–55): quarantine. In V2, stalls are better represented as time-warp segments or boundary constraints, not monstrous equations.
6. **Armspan/gravity constraints as hard rules** (p. 60–62): keep as “physical plausibility” warnings only.

- Convention invariance tests: swapping display conventions must not change canonical internal state.
- Classification tests: parameter regimes map to family labels (inspin vs antispin, unit circle constraints) (p. 60–62).
- “Physical plausibility” warnings (not hard failures): flag regimes like (|n|<1) as “requires rigid prop / violates tether constraint” (p. 61).

### 5) What not to do yet

- Don’t implement full stall equations from the paper (p. 54–55). Model stalls as time-warp or boundary constraints later.
- Don’t bake in any “behind the body” semantics until you have a body/obstacle overlay (p. 27–28).
- Don’t try to cover “all patterns possible” via a single universal parametric equation (the doc gestures at this ambition, but its own composites devolve into piecewise hacks) (p. 4, p. 51–56).
